1.1. Abstract
This document is the single authoritative rules engine specification for X Paradox X: board
sizing, standard chess pieces and their invented combat stat-blocks, the standard 52-card
ability deck, dice-based combat resolution, territory/upgrade economy, and the three victory
conditions. Every other document in this corpus that references game mechanics must cite
this document rather than restate or reinterpret its content, per 001_game_concept.txt
Section 1.15.3.

1.2. Key Words
board, chess pieces, movement, combat, dice, 52-card deck, suit, rank, territory, income,
Dominion Points, upgrade, victory condition, hallucination envelope.

1.3. Executive Summary
X Paradox X is played on a variable-size square board (minimum 4x4) by two players using
standard chess piece sets, a shared standard 52-card deck reinterpreted as an ability system
(suit = ability class, rank = magnitude), and six-sided-dice combat resolution, layered over
Monopoly-style territory ownership, income, and Dominion Points. Victory is achieved by
territory majority, King capture, or reaching a Dominion Points threshold. This document
declares, per game_specifications/0000_documentation_standards.txt Section 1.6.6, every place
where content was necessarily invented (piece combat stats, upgrade-role assignment, card
rank magnitudes) rather than sourced from prior ideation, each scoped to its specific
mechanic with an explicit envelope range and rationale.

1.4. Key Findings
1.4.1. Board size is a per-match, player-selected setting: 4x4, 6x6 (default), 8x8, or a
custom N x N, with a hard minimum of 4x4 and a requirement that the board always be square.
1.4.2. Pieces are standard chess pieces (King, Queen, Rook, Bishop, Knight, Pawn) using real
chess movement rules, each assigned an invented HP/Attack/Defence stat-block for dice-based
combat and territory capture; this is a declared hallucination-envelope invocation (Section
1.7.2).
1.4.3. The card system uses a standard 52-card deck (no jokers); suit maps to ability class
(Hearts=Defence, Spades=Attack, Diamonds=Economy, Clubs=Chaos) and rank maps to effect
magnitude, the latter being a declared hallucination-envelope invocation (Section 1.10.3).
1.4.4. Combat resolution (attacker Attack+d6 vs defender Defence+d6; win/tie/loss damage
table) is retained unchanged from the original ideation session as a frozen baseline.
1.4.5. Victory is reached by any of three independent paths: territory majority, King
capture, or a Dominion Points threshold, both scaled where noted to the selected board size.

1.5. Philosophical Preamble
Rules are the one place in this corpus where creativity must be most tightly bounded, because
the rules engine is the sole authority over state (003_game_philosophy.txt Section 1.6.1).
Where this document invents new content — chess-piece combat statistics that do not exist in
real chess, or magnitudes for playing-card ranks that do not exist in a standard deck — it
says so explicitly, in the narrow, declared hallucination envelope required by
0000_documentation_standards.txt Section 1.6.6, so that no invented number is ever mistaken
for a sourced one.

1.6. Board
1.6.1. The board is always square: N x N tiles, N selectable at match setup from {4, 6, 8,
custom}, with a hard minimum of N=4 (below which the game is judged unplayably cramped, per
explicit user direction in the 2026-07-30 clarification session). There is no stated hard
maximum, but boards are expected to remain small enough for a single-screen digital tabletop
view.
1.6.2. Tile composition scales proportionally from the N=6 baseline established in the
original ideation session (2 home territories, 10 capturable territories, 8 impassable/
unstable tiles, 16 ordinary tiles, total 36 tiles for N=6): each category is scaled to the
same approximate proportion of the new tile total (N^2) and rounded to the nearest whole
tile, with home territories fixed at exactly 2 (one per player) regardless of N.
1.6.3. The board layout remains symmetrical for both players at every supported size, per
the original ideation session's balancing guidance.

1.7. Pieces and Movement

1.7.1. Piece Roster and Movement Rules
Each player uses a standard chess piece set, scaled to board size per Section 1.7.1.1, using
unmodified real chess movement legality (orthogonal any distance for the Rook, diagonal any
distance for the Bishop, any direction any distance for the Queen, one square any direction
for the King, the L-shaped jump for the Knight, one square forward - two on its first move -
with diagonal capture for the Pawn), constrained additionally by the board edges and by any
impassable/unstable tiles defined in Section 1.6.2.
1.7.1.1. Piece-count scaling by board size:
| Board Size | King | Queen | Rook | Bishop | Knight | Pawn |
|------------|------|-------|------|--------|--------|------|
| 4x4 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 2 |
| 6x6 (default) | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 4 |
| 8x8 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 8 |
| Custom | Scaled proportionally to the nearest supported tier above, rounded down | | | | | |

1.7.2. Combat Stat-Blocks (Hallucination Envelope Declared)
Hallucination envelope invocation: context = "X Paradox X chess-piece combat stat-block";
declared range = 0.4 (moderate-low, since values must remain roughly proportionate to each
piece's real-chess power level to stay intuitive); rationale = real chess pieces carry no
native HP/Attack/Defence values, so these numbers are invented to enable dice-based combat
while preserving each piece's relative real-chess power ranking (Queen > Rook ~ Bishop >
Knight > Pawn, King precious but not weak).
| Piece | Attack | Defence | Health |
|-------|--------|---------|--------|
| King | 2 | 3 | 6 |
| Queen | 4 | 2 | 5 |
| Rook | 3 | 3 | 5 |
| Bishop | 2 | 2 | 4 |
| Knight | 3 | 2 | 4 |
| Pawn | 1 | 1 | 2 |

1.7.3. Combat Resolution
Retained unchanged from the original ideation session as a frozen baseline (not subject to
the hallucination envelope, since it is a sourced, previously agreed mechanic):
`attacker total = attacker Attack + 1d6`; `defender total = defender Defence + 1d6`. If
attacker total > defender total, the defender loses 2 HP. If equal, both lose 1 HP. If
defender total > attacker total, the attacker loses 1 HP. A piece at 0 HP is removed from
the board.

1.7.4. Upgrade-Building Role Assignment (Hallucination Envelope Declared)
Hallucination envelope invocation: context = "X Paradox X upgrade-building piece role";
declared range = 0.5 (moderate, since no real-chess precedent exists for a "builder" piece
role at all); rationale = the original ideation session assigned upgrade-building to a
bespoke "Architect" unit that no longer exists under the standard-chess-piece canon; the
Bishop is selected as its replacement because it is the least combat-dominant non-Pawn piece,
thematically suited to a support role. Only the Bishop may purchase a territory upgrade
(Section 1.9.3); this assignment is provisional and may be revisited before implementation
if it is found to under-value the Bishop in the finished balance testing described in
012_game_perfomance_tests.txt.

1.8. Turn Structure
Every turn proceeds through five phases, retained unchanged from the original ideation
session: 1) Draw one card; 2) Income - gain one credit per owned territory; 3) Move - move
one piece; 4) Action - attack, capture, build an upgrade, or play a card; 5) End Turn -
resolve effects and pass control. Move and Action may be skipped; Draw and Income may not.

1.9. Territories

1.9.1. Capture Rules
A player captures a neutral territory by ending movement on it. A player captures an
opposing territory by removing its defending piece, or by occupying it while no enemy piece
remains on the tile. Retained unchanged from the original ideation session.

1.9.2. Income and Dominion Points
Each controlled territory provides 1 credit during the Income phase. A territory held for
three consecutive turns provides 1 Dominion Point. Retained unchanged from the original
ideation session.

1.9.3. Upgrades
Only a Bishop may purchase one upgrade for its currently controlled territory (see Section
1.7.4). Each territory may hold only one upgrade.
| Upgrade | Cost | Effect |
|---------|------|--------|
| Fortress | 3 credits | Defending occupant gains +1 Defence |
| Market | 3 credits | Territory produces +1 credit |
| Relay | 2 credits | A friendly piece beginning movement here gains +1 movement range |
Retained unchanged from the original ideation session except for the piece-role restriction
in 1.7.4.

1.10. Cards

1.10.1. Standard 52-Card Deck Structure
A single standard 52-card deck (4 suits x 13 ranks, no jokers) is shared by both players as
the draw/hand deck, superseding the original ideation session's bespoke 24-card deck (see
Section 1.12).

1.10.2. Suit-to-Class Mapping
| Suit | Ability Class |
|------|-----------------|
| Hearts | Defence |
| Spades | Attack |
| Diamonds | Economy |
| Clubs | Chaos |
This mapping was explicitly specified by the user in the 2026-07-30 clarification session
and is treated as sourced, not invented.

1.10.3. Rank-to-Magnitude Mapping (Hallucination Envelope Declared)
Hallucination envelope invocation: context = "X Paradox X 52-card rank-to-magnitude
mapping"; declared range = 0.45 (moderate, since a standard deck has no native game-effect
magnitude and a full mapping must be invented, but bounded to stay proportionate to the
original ideation session's card-effect scale of roughly 1-3 units of effect); rationale =
translates a standard deck's 13 ranks into 3 usable magnitude tiers so that every card in
the shared deck resolves to a concrete, bounded effect within each suit's class.
| Rank Range | Tier | Effect Magnitude (within the drawn suit's class) |
|------------|------|-----------------------------------------------------|
| 2-6 | Low | 1 unit (e.g. 1 damage, 1 credit, 1 HP restored) |
| 7-10 | Medium | 2 units (e.g. 2 damage, 2 credits, 2 HP restored) |
| J, Q, K | High | 3 units, or one bounded special effect (e.g. reroll a die, swap two unoccupied non-home tiles) |
| Ace | Wild | Player's choice of any one tier's effect within the drawn suit's class |

1.11. Victory Conditions
A player wins immediately upon meeting any one of the following, retained in spirit from the
original ideation session with the Crown renamed to King to match the standard-chess-piece
canon:
1.11.1. Territory majority: control more than half of the board's capturable territories
(e.g. 7 of 12 at the N=6 default tile composition from Section 1.6.2).
1.11.2. King capture: the opposing King piece is reduced to 0 HP and removed from the board.
1.11.3. Dominion Points threshold: a player reaches 20 Dominion Points (default; may be
scaled proportionally to board size in a future balance revision, flagged here rather than
silently changed).
